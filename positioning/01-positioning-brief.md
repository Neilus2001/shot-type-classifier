# Positioning Brief: Filmmaker / Creator / Legacy of Folktales

Built with the Mogul Media framework (message / enemy / belonging, three content layers, and writing for objections).

---

## 1. The one sentence (pick one and repeat it for 12 months)

Options, strongest first:

1. **"Every filmmaker now has to choose: make the film, build the system, or trust someone else's."**
2. "Filmmakers are craftspeople. Tech builds systems. Creators live in between, and that's where the next filmmaker is born."
3. "I've sat in distribution. I know how the promise gets broken. So I'm building my own."

Option 1 is the strongest. It's a choice, not a complaint, and it gives every reader a place to stand, including the ones who pick "just make films."

---

## 2. Message / Enemy / Belonging

| Message | Enemy | Belonging |
|---|---|---|
| Filmmakers need to own at least one part of the system: audience, distribution, or data. | **The Rescue Promise:** the recurring pitch that a platform will build a fair system *for* you (streaming, festival markets, crowdfunding platforms, NFTs, and now AI studios). | **Folk filmmakers:** storytellers who own the fire their stories are told around. |
| Craft and systems are two jobs. Pretending they're one job burns out the artist. Pretending the craft doesn't matter produces content, not cinema. | **"Content"**: the flattening of film into inventory. | **Hybrid makers:** people who can talk to a DP and a developer in the same afternoon. |

**Don't make "tech" the enemy.** The essay argues filmmakers should consider *building systems*, and systems-building is tech. If tech is the enemy, you contradict yourself by the third paragraph. The enemy is the **promise**: the deal where someone else builds the system and you hope it pays you fairly. This is the Mogul doc's "Death of Outreach" lesson. The message was fine; the enemy was wrong.

### Why "Legacy of Folktales" works for this, if you use it on purpose

Folktales are the oldest distribution system that never needed a gatekeeper. Tellers owned the story, the audience, and the room. Each retelling was an update to the software. That makes the brand name an argument in itself:

> Folktales survived for a thousand years without a studio, a platform, or a distributor. They survived because the teller owned the relationship with the listener.

Use this as the spine of the brand. If you don't, "Legacy of Folktales" reads as a genre label and not a business thesis.

## 3. Your uncopyable proof (the "Decisions you made" layer)

Your rare asset is **distribution experience**, not "filmmaker + creator." Thousands of people claim filmmaker + creator. Very few directors have sat on the side of the table where films get bought, priced, windowed, and buried.

Fill these in. Each one is a post:

- [ ] A deal you saw where the filmmaker got paid far less than they expected, and exactly why
- [ ] The moment you realised a platform's "promise" was structurally impossible to keep
- [ ] What distributors actually look at that filmmakers never think about
- [ ] A decision you made in your own work *because* of what you saw in distribution
- [ ] Numbers from your YouTube channel that show the system working (subscribers → email list → revenue, or whatever is real)

**The last item is load-bearing.** "Poster child of what new filmmaking looks like" is a claim. Your numbers are the evidence. Without them, the essay is an opinion. With them, it's a case study.

## 4. The three content layers for this position

| Layer | Job | Examples | Scoreboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top: reach the right strangers** | Get filmmakers and film-adjacent people to see the choice | "Streaming promised you a fair wage. Here's the math on what it paid." / "Folktales had no distributor." | Qualified views, follows from filmmakers, producers, and creators |
| **Middle: filter** | Prove you understand their problem better than they can say it | Breakdowns of real distribution deals. "What I'd do with a finished feature and no distributor." Your own channel's numbers, shown openly. | Saves, replies, DMs, Substack subscribes |
| **Bottom: ask** | Whatever Legacy of Folktales sells | **Undefined. See section 6.** | Calls, sales, applications |

---

## 5. Objections to write for (each is a post)

A buyer has objections. These are the ones your audience will raise:

1. "I'm an artist. I don't want to be a marketer." → *Then choose door #1, and choose it on purpose.*
2. "Building an audience takes years and I have a film to make now."
3. "Isn't 'creator' a step down from 'filmmaker'?"
4. "Streaming and distribution still work for some people. You're just bitter."
5. "Tech people will always out-build us. Why compete?"
6. "AI will make all of this irrelevant anyway."

---

## 6. The missing information (answer before publishing)

- **What does Legacy of Folktales sell, and to whom?** Films? Consulting for filmmakers? A slate that brands or funders back? A community? The bottom layer of the funnel doesn't exist until you answer this.
- **Who is the buyer?** Filmmakers as an audience have high passion and low spending power. That's the "100,000 of the wrong ones" risk from the Mogul doc. Producers, funders, brands, and distributors have budgets.
- **How does this connect to the YouTube channel?** Same audience, adjacent audience, or a different one?
